# 905 nm vs 1535 nm laser rangefinder modules

Maintained by ERDI TECH LTD (ERDILRF). This note explains the engineering
trade-off between the two wavelength families we supply. It is not a laser safety
assessment for your product.

## Why the wavelength changes the design

| Property | 905 nm | 1535 nm |
|---|---|---|
| Detector | Silicon APD / PIN, widely available | InGaAs or similar, usually more expensive |
| Atmospheric transmission | Good in clear air, more scattering in fog/dust | Good, with different scattering behaviour |
| Eye considerations | 905 nm reaches the retina; Class 1 operation usually depends on pulse energy, repetition rate and beam expansion | 1535 nm is strongly absorbed by water in the cornea and vitreous, so the retinal hazard is much lower; corneal exposure limits still apply |
| Typical use | Short to medium range, cost-sensitive, high volume | Medium to long range where eye safety at the aperture matters |
| Source availability | Mature supply chain | Fewer suppliers, longer lead times |

The practical consequence: at the same pulse energy, 1535 nm is usually easier to
keep inside a lower laser class at the aperture. That does not make it "safe" in
the regulatory sense — the finished product still needs a classification and, in
most markets, a named safety responsible person.

## Range is not a wavelength property

Range depends on:

- transmit pulse energy and receiver sensitivity;
- target reflectivity at the chosen wavelength;
- receiver aperture and beam divergence;
- ambient light and atmospheric conditions;
- the signal-processing threshold and the accepted false-alarm rate.

A 1535 nm module does not automatically out-range a 905 nm module. Compare
measurement conditions, not the headline number. ERDI lists per-model conditions
on the model pages; for example the [LRF200H](https://erdilrf.com/models/lrf200h/)
page separates 90% and 10% reflectivity ranges, and the
[LRF0306C](https://erdilrf.com/models/lrf0306c/) page documents its 1535 nm
conditions rather than promoting a single maximum.

## When to choose 905 nm

- The target is cooperative and the working range is short to medium.
- Cost, size and supply continuity matter more than aperture eye safety.
- You already have a silicon detector supply chain and calibrated optics.
- You can keep the beam expanded or the pulse energy low enough for the required
  class, and you have the test house access to prove it.

## When to choose 1535 nm

- The aperture must stay eye-safer without a large beam expander.
- The application is long range with a small receiver aperture.
- The platform is airborne or handheld and the safety case is the limiting factor.
- You accept a higher unit cost and a narrower supplier base.

## What we do not claim

- We do not claim a laser class for a finished product.
- We do not claim that 1535 nm is "eye safe" without qualification.
- We do not publish a single range number without its target and visibility
  conditions.
- We do not treat a module datasheet as a system-level safety file.
